# PyTorch Foundation-Hosted Projects

![PyTorch Ecosystem](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/pytorch/pytorch/0d4cedaa47c7ee22042eb24e87eb3cfe95502404/docs/source/_static/img/pytorch-logo-dark.svg)

This repository is designed to support open source projects applying to become **foundation-hosted projects** under the PyTorch Foundation. It outlines the intake process, requirements, and governance path for projects seeking Incubation or Graduated status under the PyTorch Foundation.

# PyTorch Foundation \- Project Lifecycle

This lifecycle document is maintained by the PyTorch Foundation and may be amended by a ⅔ majority vote of the Technical Advisory Council (TAC) and PyTorch Foundation Governing Board.

## Goals & Principles

The goal of this document is to describe the multi-stage lifecycle for projects hosted by the PyTorch Foundation. The lifecycle is intended to be simple for project owners to understand and follow, while holding projects to a quality bar that gives users a clear signal of their maturity, stability, and resilience.

The PyTorch Foundation aims to provide clarity to ML practitioners by highlighting active projects through the PyTorch Ecosystem, hosting and guiding Incubated projects, and hosting and growing Graduated projects.

## Project Lifecycle Stages

Projects hosted with the PyTorch Foundation can be in one of three stages: Incubated, Graduated or Archived. These projects are hosted by the PyTorch Foundation using Linux Foundation neutral governance. Separately, the community maintains a list of projects in the [broader ecosystem landscape](https://github.com/pytorch-fdn/ecosystem) of AI projects and solutions that relate to the PyTorch Project. 

### Incubation Stage

The Incubation Stage is for earlier-stage projects. It aims to support the growth and sustainability of such a project by providing it with open source resources, new communication channels and direct connection to other well-established PyTorch Foundation-hosted projects.

**Requirements** 

* Use an OSI-approved permissive license for code (Apache 2.0, MIT, or 3-Clause BSD). Documentation and specification documents must use an appropriate open license (e.g., CC-BY-4.0 or the Open Web Foundation Agreement for specification documents).  
* Have a defined project mission and scope.   
* Have clear and comprehensive documentation.   
* Have defined technical governance, and appropriate README, CODEOWNERS, CONTRIBUTING and RELEASE files.  
* Remediate any license or security issues identified on an ongoing basis.  
* Demonstrate project health and stability via activity level and contributors from multiple organizations.  
* Ensure GitHub organization is standalone and clear of any non-project repositories.   
* Provide a brief quarterly project update and undergo an annual review by the PyTorch Foundation TAC. These updates should include major maintainership changes, community activities, recent releases and any major progress in the project.

**Process**

1. Find a current TAC member to sponsor and guide the application process.   
2. Submit a completed Project Contribution Proposal Template to the TAC.  
3. Present the project's proposal to the TAC. Project teams should be prepared to present a detailed (20-30 minutes in length) overview of the project and speak to the information in the contribution proposal. Including, but not limited to, details of the project scope, contributors, health and upcoming goals.  
4. Obtain a majority vote of the PyTorch Foundation TAC (“TAC”).  
5. Complete and approve the Technical Charter and transfer any relevant trademarks to the Linux Foundation or its affiliate, LF Projects, LLC, and assist in filing for any relevant unregistered ones.  
6. Enable owner access for the Linux Foundation on all project assets.  
7. Enable EasyCLA or DCO on all project repositories.   
8. Set up a Technical Steering Committee and hold public meetings at least quarterly. 

**Benefits**

* Same [benefits](https://github.com/pytorch-fdn/tac/blob/main/docs/governance/PyTorch_Ecosystem_Process.md#ecosystem-benefits) as the PyTorch Ecosystem projects.  
* Blog announcement or similar communication announcing the inclusion of the project at the Incubation Stage.  
* Marketing support for community blogs and participation opportunities at various PyTorch events.  
* Neutral hosting of the project’s community and key assets.  
* Access to the PyTorch Foundation collaboration tools (such as LFX, GitHub, mailing lists, and Slack).   
* Defense of trademarks and logos against infringement.  
* Mentorship and support from PyTorch Foundation staff and/or TAC members.

### Graduated Stage

The Graduated Stage is for established projects. It aims to provide them with the long term stability and support to continue growing their reach and provide un-paralleled resilience.

**Requirements**  
All projects at the Graduated Stage must meet the requirements from the Incubated Stage and, in addition, as determined by the TAC:

* Demonstrate a substantial ongoing flow of contributions authored by a healthy number of diverse contributors.  
* Demonstrable roadmap progress.  
* Evidence of real-world production deployments and/or significant industry adoption/market impact. 

**Process**

1. Present the project’s stage change proposal to the TAC.  
2. Obtain ⅔ majority votes of the TAC and the PyTorch Foundation Governing Board.

**Benefits**  
In addition to the benefits provided under the Incubated stage, Graduated projects receive the following: 

* Marketing support through webinars and community blogs, along with priority participation and speaking opportunities at various PyTorch events.  
* The right to appoint a voting representative to the TAC.  
* A blog announcement or similar communication announcing the graduation of the project.

### Archived Stage

Archived projects are projects that have reached end-of-life. They are no longer actively maintained, don’t have recent releases and its usage in the community has significantly diminished.

A project may be nominated for Archived at any time via opening a GitHub issue in the TAC repository. The issue should include the reason why the project should be archived and desired retirement date. The TAC can approve the move to Archived with a 2/3 vote. 

Archived projects lose access to Foundation support, marketing, and event resources, and are listed as archived on the PyTorch website. The Linux Foundation will hold all assets in perpetuity. Archived projects should clearly state their status in their repository’s README.md, indicate what users or contributors can expect regarding response times or support, and list any alternative or successor project(s). 

## Annual Project Review

In addition to quarterly updates to the TAC, each project undergoes an annual review to discuss:

* Maintainership and leadership changes  
* Community activity and growth  
* Release cadence and recent releases  
* Security posture and any open vulnerabilities  
* Progress against key objectives and roadmap milestones

Based on this review, the TAC may determine that the project remains in its current stage, recommend corrective actions, or recommend that the project be considered for transition to a different lifecycle stage.
